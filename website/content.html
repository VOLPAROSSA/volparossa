Shared work, separate permissions +

The design divides suitable tasks and combines their results. Shared learning uses permitted data, with evaluation and correction as part of development. Private prompts, files and results are not automatically shared training material.

Confidential execution of private tasks on other participants’ devices remains unfinished. A local coding result does not establish protected distributed execution.

DPS — Decentralized Positioning System

Find your way, together.

Privacy-preserving positioning

DPS is being developed to combine satellite signals, nearby devices and local sensors, with explicit permissions and clear uncertainty. The goal is to improve positioning while keeping precise locations and movement history private.

What the research establishes +

The current prototype assesses supplied position claims. Real sensor fusion, navigation and measured accuracy remain in development. Relative position does not establish an absolute location, and agreeing or signed claims do not prove geographic truth.

Receiving positioning help, accessing sensors, contributing measurements, retaining history and training models each need separate permission. Joining the network does not authorize sharing your precise location or movement history.

DPS uses the five Core layers. Privacy is a design goal, not a guarantee of anonymity. This page requests no location or sensor access; the illustrated points are fictional.

Participation is voluntary. Joining the network means accepting its resource-sharing responsibilities.

What participation means +

Participation requires explicit activation. Your own device use takes priority. Shared storage calls for a matching usable contribution, including recovery copies and overhead. No payment is required to participate.

Participants contribute according to available capabilities. Devices with an independent Internet connection also provide authorized exit service; local-only devices contribute local links and relay capacity.
